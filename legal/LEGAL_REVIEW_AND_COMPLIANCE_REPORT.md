## 2. Statutory Model Articles: Table F Analysis

Under Section 5(1) of the Companies Act, 2013, the articles of a company shall contain the regulations for the management of the company. Pursuant to Section 5(2), the articles of a company limited by shares must be in the form specified in **Table F of Schedule I**.

Section 5(6) and Section 5(7) establish that:
1. A company may adopt all or any of the regulations contained in model articles.
2. In so far as the registered articles do not exclude or modify Table F regulations, those regulations shall, so far as applicable, apply automatically by default.

### 2.1 Modifications and Exclusions of Table F Regulations
To construct a tailored, founder-protective, scalable constitution for Kissan Mithar, the following standard Table F regulations have been expressly modified or excluded:

| Table F Regulation | Subject Matter | Custom Treatment in Kissan Mithar AOA | Legal & Commercial Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reg. 1** | Model Table F Interpretation | **Modified** | Expressly states that the custom Articles apply, and Table F regulations apply only to the extent not inconsistent or modified herein. |
| **Reg. 11–18** | Share Transfer | **Modified & Substituted** | Table F contains generic transfer rules. Substituted with strict **Right of First Refusal (ROFR)**, Permitted Family Transfers, and Fair Valuation mechanism to comply with Section 2(68)(i). |
| **Reg. 48–56** | Proceedings at General Meetings | **Modified** | Tailored to private company rules; specifies quorum of 2 members personally present; sets out electronic meeting procedures under SS-2. |

## 3. Governance Analysis: The 55:45 Shareholding Architecture

The 55% (Ranjith) vs 45% (Chandra Mohan) shareholding presents unique corporate governance dynamics under the Companies Act, 2013 that must be balanced between statutory majority rule and minority founder protection.

### 3.1 Shareholder Meeting Dynamics
1. **Ordinary Resolutions (Section 114(1))**:
   - Requires votes cast in favour to exceed votes cast against (> 50%).
   - *Analysis*: With 55%, Ranjith holds an outright mathematical majority for ordinary resolutions (e.g., approval of accounts, declaration of dividends within Board recommendations, appointment of statutory auditors).
   - *Protection*: To prevent unilateral decisions on critical issues, the AOA embeds **Article 41 (Reserved Matters)** requiring the affirmative written consent of both founders for high-impact commercial actions regardless of resolution type.
2. **Special Resolutions (Section 114(2))**:
   - Requires votes cast in favour to be at least three times votes cast against ($\ge 75\%$).
   - *Analysis*: Neither Ranjith (55%) nor Chandra Mohan (45%) can pass a special resolution unilaterally. Both must agree to pass a special resolution.
   - *Chandra Mohan's Position*: Holding 45% gives Chandra Mohan an absolute **blocking minority** ($> 25\%$) against any special resolution (e.g., alteration of MOA/AOA, capital reduction, voluntary winding up, loans above limits).

### 3.2 Board Level Dynamics (2-Director Board)
1. **Quorum under Section 174(1)**:
   - The quorum for a meeting of the Board of Directors of a company shall be one-third of its total strength or two directors, whichever is higher.
   - *Impact*: In a 2-director company, **both directors must be present in person or via video conferencing** to constitute a valid quorum. If one director abstains or boycotts, no Board meeting can proceed.
2. **Board Voting & Parity**:
   - Each director holds one vote at the Board level, regardless of shareholding.
   - Ranjith (1 vote) and Chandra Mohan (1 vote) have equal voting power at the Board.
3. **The Casting Vote Decision**:
   - Under Table F Reg. 68(ii), the Chairman has a second or casting vote in case of a tie.
   - *If Included*: If Ranjith is appointed Chairman with a casting vote, Ranjith would control 100% of Board decisions during any disagreement, rendering Chandra Mohan's 45% equity powerless at Board level.
   - *If Excluded*: A 1-1 tie creates a deadlock, but forces consensus and respects co-founder parity.
   - *Legal Recommendation*: **Exclude the casting vote**. Adopt the structured multi-tier deadlock resolution protocol described below.

## 4. Multi-Tier Deadlock Resolution Protocol

To resolve board-level disagreements without resorting to destructive court litigation under Sections 241–242 (oppression and mismanagement) or winding up under Section 271, **Article 36** establishes a 4-tier escalation mechanism:

```
[Level 1: Good-Faith Founder Consultation (14 Days)]
                     │
                     ▼ (If unresolved)
[Level 2: Mediation by Mutually Agreed Neutral Senior Professional (21 Days)]
                     │
                     ▼ (If technical/valuation/accounting dispute)
[Level 3: Binding Independent Expert Determination]
                     │
                     ▼ (If fundamental deadlock persists > 45 days)
[Level 4: Shareholder Referral / SHA Exit Trigger (ROFR / Fair Valuation Buyout)]
```

### 4.1 Operation of the Protocol
1. **Tier 1 — Cooling-Off & Direct Consultation**: The disputing directors must meet informally within 14 calendar days of the deadlocked meeting to seek an amicable business solution.
2. **Tier 2 — Non-Binding Professional Mediation**: If unresolved, the dispute is referred to an agreed neutral senior professional (practising Senior Advocate, Fellow Chartered Accountant, or Fellow Company Secretary) who conducts conciliation within 21 days.
3. **Tier 3 — Expert Determination for Specific Disputes**: Where the deadlock concerns financial statements, asset valuation, or software/technology IP ownership, the decision of an independent IBBI-registered valuer or technical specialist shall be final and binding.
4. **Tier 4 — Contractual Exit Mechanism**: Where the deadlock threatens company survival, it triggers a mutual buyout procedure under the Shareholders' Agreement or enables one founder to purchase the other's shares at Fair Market Value determined by an independent valuer.

## 5. Share Transfer Restrictions & Founder Protection

Section 2(68)(i) requires private companies to restrict the right to transfer shares.

### 5.1 Right of First Refusal (ROFR) Mechanics (Article 17)
- Any shareholder wishing to sell shares ("Transferor") must serve a written **Transfer Notice** on the Board and the other shareholder ("Non-Selling Shareholder").
- The Transfer Notice specifies the number of shares, proposed price, identity of prospective buyer, and terms.
- The Non-Selling Shareholder has **30 calendar days** from receipt of notice to elect to purchase all (or part) of the Offered Shares at the proposed price or at **Fair Market Value (FMV)**.
- If the Non-Selling Shareholder declines, the Transferor may transfer to the third party within 60 days, strictly at a price not lower and on terms not more favourable than offered to the founder.

### 5.2 Fair Valuation Methodology (Article 19)
- If the transfer price is disputed, or in cases of transmission, valuation is determined by an **Independent Registered Valuer** registered under Section 247 with the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI), or an independent Practising Chartered Accountant.
- Valuation shall be conducted in accordance with internationally accepted valuation standards, applying the **Discounted Free Cash Flow (DCF)** or **Net Asset Value (NAV)** method in line with Rule 11UA of the Income-tax Rules, 1962.
- The valuer acts as an expert and not as an arbitrator. Valuation costs are borne equally.

### 5.3 Permitted Transfers (Article 18)
- A founder may transfer shares for estate planning or tax purposes to:
  1. Immediate family members (spouse, lineal ascendants, or descendants); or
  2. A private trust or corporate holding entity 100% owned and beneficially controlled by such founder.
- *Condition*: The transferring founder must retain all voting rights and remain the authorized representative for such shares.

### 5.4 Call Option on Transmission / Death (Article 22)
- In the event of death or permanent total incapacity of either founder, shares transmit to legal heirs under Section 56.
- To prevent agricultural tech operations from being paralyzed by non-active or disputing family heirs, the surviving founder is granted a **Call Option** exercisable within 180 days to acquire the deceased founder's shares at Fair Market Value determined by an Independent Registered Valuer.

1. Alteration of MOA or AOA.
2. Capital alteration, restructuring, or buy-back.
3. Further issue of shares, debentures, convertible securities, or ESOP creation.

5. Creating charges, liens, mortgages, or giving guarantees/indemnities for third parties.
6. Transfer, sale, licensing, or encumbrance of core Intellectual Property or material assets.
7. Related-party transactions under Section 188.
8. Mergers, amalgamations, joint ventures, or acquiring other businesses.
9. Material diversification outside agriculture, farmer services, agri-commerce, and technology.
10. Appointment or removal of Key Managerial Personnel (CEO, CFO, CS) or Statutory Auditors.
11. Voluntary liquidation, winding up, or IBC corporate insolvency filings.
12. Declaration or payment of dividends.

### 6.2 Entrenchment under Section 5(3) & 5(4) (Article 42)
- Under Section 5(3), articles may provide that specified provisions may be altered only if conditions more restrictive than a special resolution are met.
- Under Section 5(4), entrenchment provisions can be incorporated on formation of a private company.
- **Entrenched Articles**: Articles 16, 17 (ROFR), 36 (Deadlock & No Casting Vote), 41 (Reserved Matters), and 42 (Entrenchment) are entrenched.
- **Alteration Condition**: These articles may only be altered, amended, or repealed with the **unanimous (100%) written consent of all members** of the company.
- **MCA Compliance**: This entrenchment must be specifically declared in Form SPICe+ Part B and Form INC-34 during incorporation.

### 7.1 Agri Bazaar & Marketplace Transactions
The AOA accommodates multiple commercial models:
1. *Marketplace Operator / Intermediary*: Section 79 of the Information Technology Act, 2000 provides intermediary safe harbour, provided due diligence and terms of service are maintained.
2. *Commission Agent / Sourcing Facilitator*: Subject to the Telangana Agricultural (Produce and Livestock) Markets Act, 1966 and GST Section 15(2).
3. *Direct Principal-to-Principal Buyer and Reseller*: Requires proper state APMC trading licences, commercial invoicing, and input tax credit management.

### 7.2 Own-Brand Food Products
1. **FSSAI Licensing**: Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 requires State/Central FSSAI licence as "Manufacturer", "Marketer", or "Relabeller" for rice, grains, pulses, spices, and dry fruits.
2. **Contract Manufacturing / Private Labelling**: The manufacturing partner must hold valid manufacturing FSSAI licences, while Kissan Mithar holds Marketer/Brand Owner licences.
3. **Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011**: Mandatory declarations including Net Quantity, MRP, Unit Sale Price, Consumer Care Contact, Best Before/Use By, and Packer/Marketer details.
4. **Trademark Protection**: Registrations recommended under **Class 30** (rice, spices, flour, staples), **Class 31** (raw agricultural seeds, grains, pulses), and **Class 29** (dry fruits, edible nuts).

### 7.3 Agricultural Inputs E-Commerce (Telangana Jurisdiction)
1. **Seeds**: Retail sale and e-commerce distribution require a Seed Licence issued by the Telangana Department of Agriculture under the Seeds Act, 1966 and Seed (Control) Order, 1983.
2. **Fertilizers**: Fertilizer Retail Dealer Licence required under the Fertilizer (Inorganic, Organic or Mixed) (Control) Order, 1985 (FCO).
3. **Pesticides & Insecticides**: Highly regulated under the Insecticides Act, 1968 and Insecticides Rules, 1971. Requires licensed premises, qualified technical personnel (B.Sc. Agriculture or Chemistry), and separate retail/stockist permissions.

### 7.4 Technology, AI & Data Protection
1. **Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act)**: Kissan Mithar collects farmer personal data, phone numbers, geolocation, soil health reports, and voice interactions. The company acts as a "Data Fiduciary", requiring notice, consent, secure cloud infrastructure, and data principal access rights.
2. **Intellectual Property Protection**: Article 46 ensures that proprietary AI models, training algorithms, crop diagnostic tools, and mobile application source code are owned exclusively by the Company and assigned by founders/employees.
